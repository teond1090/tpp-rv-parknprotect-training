## Section 1 — Why This Program Exists

So what does RV Park 'n' Protect actually cover? The honest answer:
whatever's in the space. RVs, campers, trailers, boats, cars, trucks —
indoors or outdoors, covered or uncovered. If it's parked in your facility,
this plan is built for it.

Now, about that gap. Because these vehicles are driven so few days a year,
more than half of veteran owners drop their collision and comprehensive
coverage for the off season — often on their own insurer's advice! It
saves them money every month, sure. But then someone clips their trailer
on-site in January, and suddenly a minor accident is a completely
out-of-pocket expense. That's the moment this plan exists for.

And here's a piece tenants absolutely love: contents coverage starts at
three hundred fifty dollars, and it *travels*. On-site and off-site. So if
they take the camper out for the weekend and something's stolen while
they're away — still covered. Auto policies almost never do that.

One more crowd-pleaser: the second vehicle. If a tenant drives their car to
your facility, takes the RV out, and leaves the car in the space? That car
is covered too. Just have them list it on the addendum — that's exactly
what that section is for. No extra fee, no separate plan.

## Section 2 — Plans & Presenting

Let's talk tiers — and how to present them like a pro.

The example levels are one thousand, fifteen hundred, and twenty-five
hundred dollars, with contents coverage from three hundred fifty. And just
like every TPP program — those are examples, not a fixed rate card.
Coverage levels and pricing are flexible, and we can accommodate most
customer needs and requests. Something different? Just ask.

Now here's the pitch that sells itself. A tenant tells you their auto
deductible is about a thousand dollars. You tell them: for around thirteen
dollars a month, this plan covers a thousand dollars of damage. Which
means — when something happens, the plan effectively pays their deductible
for them. Thirteen dollars a month to never eat a thousand-dollar
deductible. That's the whole conversation!

And when you offer it, ask *which*, not *whether*. "Would you like a
protection plan?" invites a no. "Which level of protection would you like?"
moves things forward. And look at what they're actually parking — a fishing
boat and a forty-foot motorhome are two very different conversations, and
tenants notice when you notice.

Don't forget the addendum: it records the coverage level, it's where every
vehicle in the space gets listed — including that second one — and it's
what claims are validated against. Signed at the time of rental. Every
time.

## Section 3 — Coverage & Exclusions

What's covered? The everyday stuff that actually happens: on-site
accidental damage — dings, dents, and collision while it's in your space.
Fire, explosion, and smoke. Burglary damage. Vandalism. Building collapse.
And theft of contents.

Here's a modern one: stolen attached parts. Somebody takes a catalytic
converter off a stored vehicle — that part was permanently attached, so
it's eligible for coverage up to the plan sublimit. The part itself, not
the labor to install it. Good to know before a tenant asks, because these
days, they ask.

Now the exclusions. Rodent and critter damage is excluded — these vehicles
sit outdoors in non-climate-controlled spaces, so that one's deliberate.
Also excluded: flood, mysterious disappearance, and mold. And one to watch:
fire and explosion are covered, but *wildfires* are specifically excluded.
Know that line before fire season.

Burglary has a hard rule: no claim is paid without a police report. The
tenant files with the police and provides a copy, the claim needs photos of
the visible forced entry, and you provide your incident report as the
manager. Tenant calls about a break-in? First words: "Call the police."

And the tenant announcement letters — easy. You approve them first; nothing
reaches a tenant until you've signed off. Depending on your program setup,
we provide them finished and ready to send, or as files your team uses to
produce them. Either way, they're sent from your own property management
software, so they arrive from a name your tenants already recognize.

## Section 4 — Filing a Claim

Something happened. Here's exactly how a claim runs.

All claims are filed online at rv-park-n-protect-claims-dot-com, any hour
of any day. The window is thirty days from the date of *discovery* — not
from when the damage happened. Big difference when a tenant hasn't checked
on their boat in two months.

Tell tenants to start early. Open the claim immediately, get repair quotes
going right away, and send documentation in as it comes — quotes need to
arrive before that thirty-day window closes, and waiting until everything
is gathered is how people miss it. The tenant gathers their own receipts
and documentation and submits their own claim; your part is the incident
report and supporting paperwork.

Once a claim is open, keep every conversation about it inside the claims
portal, not email. The portal keeps everything in the claim file, where the
adjusters can actually see it.

Here's what to expect: the moment a claim is submitted, a claim number is
issued and the tenant gets an automatic confirmation email — and that's the
one automatic email in the process. From there, updates come from the
adjuster as the claim moves, and anyone with claims portal access can check
status in the portal any time.

Live adjusters are on Monday through Friday, seven A-M to five P-M Arizona
time. And when the tenant asks when they'll be paid: processing can take up
to thirty days, though many claims are completed sooner. Never promise a
payment date, and never estimate a reimbursement amount — even to be
helpful. "I'm not the right person to put a number on that, but the
adjuster will walk you through it." Perfect answer. Use it word for word.
